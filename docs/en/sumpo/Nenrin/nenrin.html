Nenrin User Guide

Era years, zodiac and ages — a reference table you can annotate

In Japan, years are counted two ways at once. 2026 is also Reiwa 8 — and official forms, licences, certificates, museum labels, and gravestones frequently use only the second form. Nenrin puts both on the same line, for every year from 1600 to 2100.

Each row shows the Western year, the Japanese era year, the zodiac animal, and an age. You can read the table in any direction: era year to Western year, age to birth year, or birth year to age. Optional columns add the cultural markers Japanese people look for in a year — the sexagenary cycle, the nine stars, school milestones, longevity celebrations, and unlucky years.

The name comes from 年輪 nenrin, the growth rings of a tree — the year-by-year record a trunk keeps of its own life. The app's Japanese name is 和暦年齢メモ (wareki nenrei memo), "era-year and age notes".

📝 The interface is in English, but the calendar data stays in Japanese. Era names, zodiac signs, rokuyo and the rest are shown as you will meet them on real documents — translating them would lose the information. Buttons, settings and help text are in English, and every Japanese term is given below with its reading and meaning.

Background: how Japanese years work

Era years (年号 nengō / 和暦 wareki)

The idea Japan numbers its years from the accession of each emperor. When a new emperor takes the throne, the count restarts at 1. The current era, Reiwa (令和), began in 2019 — so 2019 is Reiwa 1, and 2026 is Reiwa 8.
Year 1 is special The first year of an era is not written "1". It is written 元年 (gannen), "origin year". You will see 令和元年, not 令和1年. Nenrin follows this convention.
Transition years An era changes mid-year, so one calendar year can carry two era names. 1868 is both 慶応4年 (Keiō 4) and 明治元年 (Meiji 1), split at the date of the change. Nenrin shows both names on such rows, and marks the changeover day in that year's calendar. There are 40 such years between 1600 and 2100.
The four modern eras
  • 明治 Meiji — 1868–1912
  • 大正 Taishō — 1912–1926
  • 昭和 Shōwa — 1926–1989  (the era most older Japanese people were born in)
  • 平成 Heisei — 1989–2019
  • ★ 令和 Reiwa — 2019–  (current)
Before Meiji, eras changed far more often — for auspicious omens, disasters, or a new sixty-year cycle. The Edo period alone has dozens. Nenrin covers all of them back to 1600 (慶長 Keichō).
Quick conversion Western year = first year of the era + era year − 1
  • Shōwa → add 1925  (Shōwa 47 = 1972)
  • Heisei → add 1988  (Heisei 12 = 2000)
  • Reiwa → add 2018  (Reiwa 8 = 2026)

The zodiac (干支 eto)

Twelve animals Every year carries one of twelve animals: 🐭 rat, 🐮 ox, 🐯 tiger, 🐰 rabbit, 🐲 dragon, 🐍 snake, 🐴 horse, 🐑 sheep, 🐵 monkey, 🐔 rooster, 🐶 dog, 🐷 boar. They repeat every twelve years, and Japanese people commonly know their own animal. New Year cards are decorated with the coming year's animal.
Sixty-year cycle The full traditional name pairs the animal with one of ten "heavenly stems", giving a cycle of sixty years. 2026 is 丙午 hinoe-uma, "fire horse". The list shows the animal; open a year to see the full stem-and-branch name with its reading.

Counting age (数え年 kazoedoshi)

Two systems Modern Japan uses 満年齢 (man-nenrei) — the same as the Western count, incremented on your birthday. Traditional customs use 数え年 (kazoedoshi): you are 1 at birth, and everyone gains a year together on New Year's Day. It runs one to two years ahead of the Western count.
Why it matters Longevity celebrations and unlucky years are traditionally reckoned in kazoedoshi. Nenrin lets you switch the whole table between the two, and adjusts those markers accordingly.

Basic Flow

1 Open the list

The app starts with the current year centred on screen. Scroll up and down through the years.

2 Choose whose age

The three tabs at the bottom decide who the age column refers to: anyone, yourself, or someone on your list.

3 Jump to a year

Tap 移動 at the top right to jump by age, Western year, or era year.

4 Open a year

Tap any row to see that year's calendar, its details, and a note field.

A row in the list

Always shown Western year · Japanese era year · age. On a transition year the era column carries both era names on two lines.
Optional Zodiac animal, nine star, school milestone, longevity celebration, unlucky year — each switched on individually in Settings.
Highlights The current year and the reference person's birth year are tinted. A year you jumped to is marked in a different colour so you can see where you landed.
Negative ages Years before the reference birth year show a negative age such as -2歳, dimmed. It reads as "two years before this person was born".

The three tabs

年齢 Age Shows how old someone born in each year is today. No birth date needed. The age column moves to the left edge, which makes it easy to scan from an age to a birth year.
自分 Myself Uses the birth date you saved in Settings and shows how old you were, or will be, in each year. If no birth date is set, the settings sheet opens first.
名簿 Roster Choose a birthday to see that person's age in every year, or choose an anniversary to see its year-by-year count.

How to

Convert an era year to a Western year

Steps Tap 移動 (top right). Use the dropdown on the left to pick the era, then type the year on the keypad. The converted Western year appears on the right as you type, so you have your answer before you even jump.
Where you meet this Japanese driving licences, residence cards, bank forms, and koseki family registers are all dated in era years. So are museum captions and historical markers.
Years that never existed The app will happily convert Shōwa 100 (= 2025) or Reiwa −20 (= 1998). Japanese people often extend a closed era this way when talking about age — "he'd be Shōwa 100 this year".
Out of range If the result falls outside 1600–2100, the app says so and moves to the nearest end of the table.

Find the birth year for an age

Steps Select the 年齢 tab at the bottom — the age column moves to the left edge. Scroll, or tap 移動 and choose 年齢 from the dropdown and type the age.
Example Enter 48 and the list moves to the year someone aged 48 was born — 1978, Shōwa 53, if the current year is 2026.

Find someone's zodiac animal

Steps Turn on 干支 in Settings to show the animal in the list, then scroll to their birth year — or tap the row to see the full sixty-cycle name with its Japanese reading.
Useful for New Year cards, gifts, and the very common Japanese small-talk question 何年生まれ？ ("which animal year were you born in?"), which asks age without asking age.

Use the roster

Add Open the 名簿 tab, tap +, and choose Birthday or Anniversary. Enter a name or title (up to 20 characters) and a date; a birthday can also include a gender.
Select Tap a birthday to show that person's age table, or tap an anniversary to show its count in every year.
Counting anniversaries The starting year is year 0 and the next year is the first anniversary. The count stays the same throughout each calendar year, regardless of month and day.
Edit or delete Use the edit button at the right of the row, or swipe the row left. Deletion asks for confirmation and cannot be undone.
Reorder Press and hold a row, then drag it up or down. The order is saved.

Write a note

Write Tap a row to open the year, and use the note field in the middle. There is no save button — text is saved automatically.
Delete Clear the text and the note for that year is removed.
Hide With "メモは自分だけに表示" on in Settings, notes appear only in the 自分 view.

The cultural markers

賀寿 — longevity celebrations

What it is Certain ages call for a family celebration. The best known is 還暦 kanreki, when the sixty-year zodiac cycle returns to the animal and stem of your birth — a symbolic rebirth, traditionally marked by wearing a red vest and cap. The names that follow come from playful readings of the kanji: 米寿 beiju (88) because 米 splits into 八十八.
The ages
  • 還暦 kanreki 61 — the zodiac cycle completes
  • 緑寿 rokuju 66
  • 古希 koki 70 — "rare since antiquity"
  • 喜寿 kiju 77
  • 傘寿 sanju 80  · 半寿 hanju 81
  • 米寿 beiju 88
  • 卒寿 sotsuju 90  · 白寿 hakuju 99
  • 百寿 momoju 100  · 茶寿 chaju 108  · 皇寿 kōju 111
  • 大還暦 daikanreki 121 — a second full cycle
All are traditional kazoedoshi ages; in the Western count they fall one year earlier. Nenrin places them correctly for whichever counting method you have selected.

厄年 — unlucky years (yakudoshi)

What it is Ages considered prone to misfortune, when many people visit a shrine or temple for a purification rite (厄払い yakubarai) at the start of the year. The year before and after each unlucky year are also watched, as 前厄 maeyaku and 後厄 atoyaku.
The ages Men: 25, 42, 61. Women: 19, 33, 37, 61 — all in kazoedoshi. Men's 42 and women's 33 are 大厄 taiyaku, the great unlucky years, partly because 42 can be read shi-ni ("to death") and 33 san-zan ("disastrous"). The app requires a gender setting to show these; leave it unspecified and the column stays empty.

六曜 — the six-day cycle (rokuyo)

What it is A repeating six-day luck cycle printed on most Japanese calendars and consulted when scheduling weddings and funerals. It runs on the old lunisolar calendar, which is why the sequence jumps at the start of each lunar month.
The six days
  • 大安 taian — most auspicious; the day couples choose to marry
  • 友引 tomobiki — "pulling friends"; funerals are avoided, as it may draw others along
  • 先勝 senshō — morning good, afternoon bad
  • 先負 senbu — morning bad, afternoon good
  • 赤口 shakkō — only around noon is safe
  • 仏滅 butsumetsu — least auspicious; wedding halls discount their rates

九星気学 — nine star astrology

What it is A divination system that assigns everyone a 本命星 honmeisei, one of nine stars, from their birth year — used for fortune-telling and choosing auspicious directions to travel. The nine run from 一白水星 ippaku suisei to 九紫火星 kyūshi kasei.
Note The star year turns at 立春 risshun, the traditional start of spring in early February — not on 1 January. People born in January or early February belong to the previous year's star.

学齢 — school milestones

What it is The Japanese school year runs April to March, and children enter elementary school in the April after turning six. Because the cohort is defined by the school year rather than the birthday, Japanese people identify their peers by school year as readily as by age. The app marks elementary entry, elementary and junior high graduation, high school graduation, and university graduation.

Specifications

Calendar calculations

Range 1600 to 2100 (501 years) — Keichō 5 through Reiwa 82
Eras Taken from the operating system's Japanese calendar. The range contains 40 transition years, each showing two era names.
Zodiac Stems and branches computed from the Western year (1600 = 庚子, 1868 = 戊辰, 1926 = 丙寅, 2026 = 丙午, 2100 = 庚申)
Rokuyo Derived from the lunisolar month and day. 大安 taian is highlighted in red.
Moon age The percentage is the illuminated fraction of the moon's face — 0% at new moon, 100% at full. It is calculated for the moon as it stands in the sky from evening into night, so it matches roughly what you would see that night. A full moon (100%) is highlighted in yellow. Being an approximation from the lunisolar calendar, it can be a day off from the true full moon.
Nine star Reckoned from risshun. The exact date shifts between 3 and 5 February depending on the year; the app uses the conventional 4 February.
Longevity ages 61, 66, 70, 77, 80, 81, 88, 90, 99, 100, 108, 111, 121 in kazoedoshi
Unlucky ages Men 25, 42, 61; women 19, 33, 37, 61 in kazoedoshi, with the years either side as maeyaku and atoyaku
